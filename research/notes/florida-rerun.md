## 1. The question

C1 (`research/checkpoints/C1.md`) answered RQ1 and RQ2 on the 9-asset thesis portfolio, and MAR-182 (`research/notes/c1-combination.md`) the combination; both said the thesis portfolio is a weak test of joint calibration (C1.md 7b, item 5; observation 33). This note reruns the three views on the paper portfolio D2, Florida's 67 counties with the State of Florida as the single owner, for both indices:

- **RQ1 (tail):** within each scope, does the weighted-CVaR design lower the owner's tail shortfall against the MSE and E[U] designs, out of sample?
- **RQ2 (joint calibration):** within each objective, does calibrating the 67 counties jointly (portfolio scope, or cluster scope with a cluster index) lower the owner's total shortfall against calibrating each county on its own, at the same budget? With the cluster index separated from the joint calibration and, under the load, the portfolio premium split into cross-coverage and pricing diversification.
- **Combination:** a portfolio-scope weighted-CVaR design against individual MSE, individual weighted CVaR and portfolio MSE, and the interaction (does the tail gain depend on the scope, and the joint gain on the objective?).

### 9.9 Coverage gaps and limitations

- **Near-optimal ranges: holdout, pure, κ 1, and not the E[U] portfolio designs.** The CoC r 0.06 and κ 0.5 / 1.5 joint designs have their second optima and free-bin ends, not their near-optimal sets; the k-fold fold designs likewise. The pure, κ 1 portfolio E[U] designs of both indices have their second optima and free-bin ends only: on the E[U] near-optimal sets each linear program took minutes (a descent-only run of the index (a) design needed 35 min for its first KPI and was stopped), so contrasts with a portfolio E[U] term have no near-optimal variant.
- **Individual designs: the free-bin method only** (exact; for MSE the whole optimal set, observation 38). MAR-198's near-optimal method (b) for the individual LP designs was not run: on the thesis portfolio it took 1–2 h per design for 9 assets, and its joint minimum program holds one near-optimal constraint per county over 70,000 (index (a)) or 97,000 (index (b)) train outcomes.
- **Per-county payout ranges:** measured for the 8 cluster designs and the 2 portfolio MSE designs (pure, κ 1). Not for the 6 portfolio E[U] and weighted-CVaR designs: each needs 134 linear programs over its near-optimal set, which took from 0.2 s to over 8 min each on this machine (warm-started HiGHS, then a fresh solve), and the runs were stopped.
- **Inner bounds.** Every near-optimal end is a checked member found by the programs; the true extremes can lie further out. HiGHS's convex program for the smallest KPI returned `optimal` for points whose recomputed KPI lay above the stored design's (its reported objective did not equal the recomputed KPI; MAR-198 saw the same), so the smallest value is taken over every checked member found, the Frank-Wolfe descent's included. The convex program also stalled on several index (a) and (b) designs: the last designs ranged (index (a) portfolio weighted CVaR β 0.995 and the stopped E[U] run) use the descent alone.
